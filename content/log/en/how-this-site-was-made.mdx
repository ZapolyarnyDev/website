## Why do I need this website?

By the autumn of last year, when I got my previous job, all my searches for side jobs came down to
endless browsing of forums and the internet. Around September, someone noticed me, and that's how I joined
my first long-term project, MineBand.

After that, all my other jobs and commissions came through acquaintances and similar routes. Over time, I found this extremely inefficient:
the impression of a developer formed through messages alone isn't as strong, is it?
So I decided to create my own website to help me in the future. This is a long-term project that isn't intended to bring quick returns.

Besides, there are things I'd like to talk about outside my Telegram diary channel (which only has the people I trust most),
because I only write about the most important things there.

> `Main goal` — make it easier to present my experience as a developer

## How did I arrive at the site's technical foundation?

I really love looking at other developers' personal pages on the internet.
At one point, I came across something called webrings for individual groups of developers

Yes, it turns out that developers (more often in open source) like creating these. This approach gives many people
a way to quickly share their skills within a particular group or community (it made it easier for me to learn from other people's projects).

I decided to study the source code of many pages (mostly by Western programmers).

I arrived at a pretty good pattern on the code side:

- Astro;
- MDX content;
- static generation;
- a convenient content parsing engine;
- minimal client-side JavaScript.

On the design side:

- a quick understanding of the developer's experience and skills;
- their interests;
- their originality;
- preferably a memorable combination of style and color palette.

## Visual direction

When it came to design, all hell broke loose. Many people are very tired of minimalism,
and some even often start seeing it as AI slop.

Remembering my graphic design classes from the previous year, I decided to outline three potential candidates:

- Brutalism;
- Neo-constructivism;
- Deconstructivism.

The last one was dropped almost immediately, since all my sketches showed extremely little appeal to a potential
visitor. I couldn't reconcile Neo-constructivism with my chosen color palette either, so that was dropped too.

### Brutalism

After several iterations, I achieved good website templates in a brutalist style. After sending them to people I knew,
I realized that this would appeal more strongly to the potential target audience, and started development!

### Color palette

There wasn't much choice. Blue didn't fit the radical layout of my templates, since they were originally designed
as an attempt to breathe more life into the world of dull minimalism.

At the time of the idea, I really liked red, so I decided to go with it.
